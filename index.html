When I'm typically brought in

Situations where I begin working

I am involved when delivery is underway, expectations are high and greater clarity is needed — without slowing execution.

01
Program scale has outgrown governance

A major program or portfolio is behind schedule, but the root cause is not clearly understood at leadership level. New structures are needed to maintain oversight.

02
Decision forums exist but are not working

Governance structures are in place, but decisions are slow, revisited or consistently avoided. Leadership needs structured clarity, not more reporting data.

03
Priorities compete without resolution

Initiatives compete for the same resources and no clear mechanism exists to make trade-offs explicit and binding. Resource friction is increasing.

04
Leadership visibility is degraded

Leaders see activity and effort, but progress on what matters most — risks, dependencies, strategic milestones — remains unclear due to growth, reorganization or turnover.

05
A transformation has stalled

A strategic initiative or transformation program is not progressing as expected. The organization is not sure why. Accountabilities and structural gaps are unclear.

06
Operational pressure is overriding strategy

Day-to-day demands are consistently displacing structured delivery and strategic execution. Governance models need rapid establishment or stabilization.

How I work

Three steps. No additional process layers.

The goal is not to add complexity. It is to create the structural clarity needed to move forward.

1

Diagnose what is actually happening

Most structural problems are visible in governance patterns and portfolio data before they surface as crises. I map the real picture across programs, dependencies, decisions and accountabilities — not the reported one. This means working from facts, not assumptions, and understanding what leadership visibility is currently missing and why.

2

Identify where structure is missing or broken

Governance gaps, undefined decision rights, missing escalation paths, unclear ownership — I identify where the structural failures are and what they are costing the organization in concrete terms. This is the foundation for targeted action rather than broad intervention.

3

Build the conditions for forward movement

Targeted structural changes: clearer governance, sharper prioritization, better decision forums and leadership visibility that accurately reflects what is happening. I build structures and internal capability that sustain beyond the engagement — grounded in how the organization actually operates, not in a generic framework.

How I typically support organizations

Engagements are scoped to the situation. Most begin with a short diagnostic conversation to establish what is actually happening before any scope is defined.

Program & Portfolio Diagnostic
Most common starting point
When it applies

A major program, portfolio or transformation is not delivering as expected. An independent, structured view of why is needed — without disrupting ongoing execution.

Typically triggered when internal efforts to understand the problem have not produced a clear answer or when leadership needs an outside perspective before deciding on next steps.

Typical scope & format

Root cause analysis across programs, governance structure, prioritization and dependency mapping. Structured findings with recommended actions.

Delivered as a focused engagement with defined output. Duration depends on scope and complexity.

Governance & Execution Support
When it applies

Leadership teams need to build or rebuild the structures required to deliver complex initiatives. Decision forums exist but are not functioning. Operating rhythms, escalation paths or accountability frameworks are missing or unclear.

Typical scope & format

Portfolio governance design, PMO structure, decision-making frameworks, program operating model, prioritization and capacity alignment.

Advisory and hands-on support. Duration and capacity scoped to the engagement and organizational rhythm.

Organizational & Cross-Functional Alignment
When it applies

Competing priorities, unclear roles and fragmented accountability are preventing execution across functions or stakeholder groups. Delivery challenges are driven by both structural gaps and unclear ownership.

Typical scope & format

Role and accountability clarification, priority resolution, cross-functional operating model, facilitated alignment sessions.

Combination of structured workshops and ongoing advisory support. Targeted interventions anchored in current work, not generic training.
